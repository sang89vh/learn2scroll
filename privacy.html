Privacy Policy

No account, no analytics, no ads. The only thing ever sent is what you type into the optional cloud AI — after you say yes.

Last updated 5 October 2026. Applies to the ScoreQuest iPhone app and this website.

Android: the sections from "ScoreQuest on Android" down to "Changes" describe the ScoreQuest Android app and were added on 17 October 2026. The sections before them describe the iPhone app.

Android, 23 October 2026: a note was added at the start of "Cloud AI, purchases and what our server keeps": ScoreQuest for Android 0.1.0 does not switch the cloud AI on.

The short version

There is no account to create, no analytics and no advertising code in the app. Everything ScoreQuest needs to work — what you block, your study progress, your earned minutes — is stored on your iPhone and stays there.

There is one exception, and it is entirely your choice. On iPhones that cannot run Apple Intelligence (iPhone 14 and older), the AI features — the tutor, the explanation of a wrong answer, and full dictionary entries — can instead be answered by OpenAI through our own server. That is switched off until you read a consent screen and agree to it, and cloud requests are metered by monthly AI Credits — from an AI plan subscription or the allowance included with the one-time Pro purchase. When it is on, we send the text you typed together with Apple's signed purchase receipt proving this device's entitlement — and nothing else about you: no account, no name, no email, no device identifier. We could not link it to you if we wanted to, because the app has never asked who you are.

In Apple's App Store privacy terms, ScoreQuest's label is Data Not Linked to You, covering user content and purchase information. On an iPhone 15 Pro or newer, the AI runs on the device or, where Apple makes it available, on Apple's Private Cloud Compute: Apple's own servers for Apple Intelligence, which do not keep what you type. Nothing is sent to us or to OpenAI from those iPhones.

What ScoreQuest stores on your iPhone

All of the following is local. It is not readable by us, and none of it is ever sent anywhere — including when the optional cloud AI features above are switched on, which send only the text you type (plus Apple's signed purchase receipt) and never this data.

  • Which apps you chose to block. Stored as the anonymous tokens iOS gives us — see the Screen Time section below.
  • Your sessions and schedules. When a block starts and ends, the strictness level you picked, and any recurring schedule you set.
  • One number: how many times you hit your own block screen. Counted per day, so the app can show you when the urge tends to arrive. It records that a block screen was reached, not which app you reached for.
  • Your settings and your purchase state. Purchases — the one-time Pro unlock and the optional AI plan subscriptions — are held against your Apple Account by Apple; the app stores locally whether they are active, plus the AI Credit balance our server reports.
  • Your wallet balance and earning history. How many minutes you have earned and spent, and which source each credit came from — a vocabulary quiz, a learning app, steps, or a focus session — so the app can enforce its daily and balance caps. There is no passcode or unlock code of any kind: nothing like that exists in ScoreQuest, and nobody but you is ever involved in getting back into a blocked app.

Screen Time data

ScoreQuest uses Apple's Screen Time frameworks (FamilyControls, ManagedSettings and DeviceActivity) for one purpose: applying the blocks you asked for.

When you choose apps to block, iOS presents its own picker and returns opaque tokens. ScoreQuest never sees app names, bundle identifiers, or a list of what is installed on your iPhone, and never tries to work them out. The tokens are meaningless outside your device.

ScoreQuest also does not request Apple's App and Website Usage permission and does not include a usage-report extension. It never sees a breakdown of how long you spend in any specific app, and it never learns app names through this channel.

There is one narrower exception, and it is opt-in: if you choose to earn minutes by spending time in apps you have marked as learning apps, iOS notifies ScoreQuest when your cumulative time in that self-chosen group crosses each 10-minute mark during the day. This is a threshold crossing, not an exact duration, and it is tied to the same anonymous tokens as everything else in this section — never an app name. It applies only to the apps you deliberately picked for learning. The apps you are blocking are never measured this way; blocking runs entirely on a schedule, never on how much you have used anything.

No information obtained through these frameworks is used for advertising, profiling, or shared with anyone — nor will it be. All of it stays on your device, exactly like everything else described on this page.

Health data

ScoreQuest can optionally read your step count from Apple Health, as one more way to earn minutes. This is a Pro feature: the app never asks for Health access unless you turn steps on as an earn source.

Access is read-only — ScoreQuest cannot write to Health, and never asks to. It reads your cumulative step count for the current day only, and it deliberately excludes any step entry you typed into the Health app by hand, counting only steps recorded automatically by a phone or watch. This exists to stop manually-entered numbers from earning minutes they were not actually walked for.

ScoreQuest does not store your raw step count or history. It checks Health at the moment it needs to, works out how many 1,000-step increments have already been credited, and keeps only that running tally locally — the same as every other wallet source on this page. Your step data is never transmitted anywhere, never included in analytics (there are none), and never shared with anyone. Deleting ScoreQuest does not delete anything from your Health app, because ScoreQuest never held a copy of it to begin with — it only ever read it in passing.

Purchases

Payment is handled entirely by Apple through the App Store. ScoreQuest never sees your card details, your name, or your Apple Account.

The optional AI plans are monthly subscriptions, and metering their cloud AI Credits takes one extra step: when you use cloud AI, the app sends Apple's signed purchase receipt to our server, which verifies it and keeps a credit ledger — how many credits this subscription has used this month — keyed to the anonymous transaction identifier inside that receipt. Apple also notifies our server when a subscription renews, is refunded or expires, so the allowance stays correct. That identifier is issued by Apple for this purchase alone; it is not your name, email, Apple Account or device identifier, and we have no way to turn it into one. If you never use cloud AI, our server never hears from your device at all.

Third parties

The app contains no third-party libraries, SDKs, trackers, crash reporters or advertising networks. One third party is involved only when you use cloud AI: the text you typed is processed by OpenAI on our behalf to generate the answer, under the consent described above. Nothing about you is ever sold, shared or brokered.

Children

ScoreQuest is designed for adults restricting their own device, and asks iOS for individual authorization rather than parental control over someone else's iPhone. It is not directed to children. Since the app collects nothing from anyone, it collects nothing from children either.

Deleting your data

Delete the app. That removes everything described above from your iPhone. There is no copy of it anywhere else, so there is nothing to request, export or erase on our side — and no way for us to do so even if you asked.

This website

These pages are static files hosted on GitHub Pages. There are no cookies, no analytics and no tracking scripts. GitHub may log requests as part of operating its service; see the GitHub Privacy Statement.

AI Role-play (speaking practice)

Role-play lets you hold a spoken conversation with an AI partner. It is only offered on iPhones that run Apple Intelligence.

  • Your voice never leaves your iPhone. The microphone is on only after you tap it, and speech is turned into text on the device by Apple's speech recognition. No audio is recorded or stored.
  • On-device mode keeps the whole conversation on your iPhone.
  • Cloud mode, when it is available, sends the conversation text to Apple's Private Cloud Compute, Apple's own private servers for Apple Intelligence. It never goes to us or to any other company. The screen before each conversation shows which mode is in use.
  • Conversations and reports are saved on your iPhone, so you can review them under History. You can delete one session or all of them at any time. Nothing you said is used to build a profile beyond simple counts kept on the device (your level and which kinds of mistakes are most common).

ScoreQuest on Android

The Android app has no account, no sign-in, no advertising, no analytics and no crash reporting of its own. What you block, your study progress, your earned minutes and the apps you chose are stored on your phone. ScoreQuest itself sends nothing anywhere unless you choose the optional cloud AI, and then only what is described under "Cloud AI, purchases and what our server keeps" below. Two other things are outside ScoreQuest's own code and are described below as well: Google Play and Google's purchase library, and Android's own backup.

The iPhone sections above speak of Apple's Screen Time, HealthKit and App Store. On Android the equivalents are the Android permissions below, Health Connect and Google Play.

What ScoreQuest stores on your Android phone

All of the following is kept in the app's private storage on your phone. It is not readable by us. The only parts of it that can leave the phone are the ones listed under "Cloud AI, purchases and what our server keeps" below.

  • The apps you chose. The apps you block, the learning apps whose study time earns minutes, and the groups you make on the Learning goals screen are stored as the apps' package names. To let you pick apps, ScoreQuest asks Android which apps have a launcher icon; that list is shown on screen and is not stored. It does not ask for the broad permission to see every installed app, and it does not use Android's Accessibility service.
  • Your sessions and schedules. When a block starts and ends, the strictness level you picked, and any recurring schedule you set.
  • One number a day: how many times you hit your own block screen. It records that a block screen was reached, not which app you reached for.
  • Your wallet and earning history. How many minutes you have earned and spent, and which source each came from (a quiz, a learning app, steps or a focus session), so the app can enforce its daily and balance caps.
  • Your settings, your learning progress and your purchase state. Whether Pro is active is kept as an encrypted copy of what Google Play last told the app you own, so the app works offline; the copy stays on this phone.

Usage access, display over other apps, and alarms

Blocking needs Android permissions that only you can grant, each on its own Android settings page after ScoreQuest has explained it. You can turn any of them off at any time, and studying works without them.

  • Usage access. Android lets an app you authorise read the phone's usage log (which app was open, and when). ScoreQuest reads it on your phone only, for two purposes: while a study window or focus session is running, to see which app is in front so it can cover the apps you chose to block; and to add up the minutes you spend in the learning apps you picked. The log is read and folded in memory on the phone. ScoreQuest does not copy it, store it or send it; what it keeps is the apps you chose, a running minute count and your earned-minutes balance.
  • Display over other apps. Lets ScoreQuest start its block screen on top of a blocked app. ScoreQuest never draws an overlay window.
  • Alarms & reminders. Lets a study window start and end on the minute. Without it a window can start late.
  • Notifications. Show that blocking is on and tell you when a study window starts or a focus session ends.

Health data on Android

ScoreQuest can optionally read your step count from Health Connect, as one more way to earn minutes. This is a Pro feature: the app asks for access only when you tap "Connect Health".

Access is read-only and for steps alone (the Health Connect permission READ_STEPS): ScoreQuest never writes to Health Connect. It reads today's step total each time you open the app, leaves out steps typed in by hand, and credits minutes for each full 1,000 steps. The step count is held in memory while the app runs; it is never stored, logged, backed up or sent anywhere, including to the cloud AI. The app stores only the minutes you earned, how many 1,000-step blocks it has already paid today, and the time span it last read. To stop, open Health Connect, choose App permissions, then ScoreQuest, and turn off Steps; the minutes you have already earned stay in your wallet. Deleting ScoreQuest deletes nothing from Health Connect.

Cloud AI, purchases and what our server keeps

In ScoreQuest for Android 0.1.0 the cloud AI is not switched on yet: its screens say AI is not available, so the app sends nothing to our server or to OpenAI, and our server keeps nothing about you. The next three paragraphs describe what will happen once a later version switches it on and you have agreed to its consent screen; the last one, about Google Play purchases, applies already.

The AI features (the tutor, answer explanations, the dictionary and the writing helpers) can be answered by OpenAI through our own server. They are off until you agree to a consent screen that says what is sent, and they need a Pro purchase and an internet connection. When one runs, the app sends, over HTTPS only: the text you asked about (your question and the last few exchanges, the word or sentence you looked up, the words of a story or reading you asked for, the week's study figures, or the answer you gave to a grammar question together with your estimated and target TOEIC scores), the Google Play purchase token and product identifiers that prove you own Pro, the name of the operation, and a random request identifier. It sends no account, name, email, device identifier or advertising identifier, nothing about which apps you use or have installed, and no step count.

On our server we keep: a credit account and a ledger of AI Credits used (counts, costs and times), filed under an identifier made from a hash of your purchase token; a hash of the token and the state of the purchase; and, for each request, a record of the answer that was generated, so a repeated request can be answered again without charging you twice. We do not store the text you typed, but the answer can repeat parts of it. Each such record is given an expiry time 24 hours after the request (when expired records are physically deleted depends on a setting of our database, and we do not promise a deletion time). The hosting platform may also log the network address of each call. Nothing is sold or used for advertising.

OpenAI processes the text on our behalf to generate the answer. OpenAI's published API documentation (read on 17 October 2026) says that data sent to its API is not used to train its models unless a developer opts in, and that abuse-monitoring logs are kept for up to 30 days by default.

Payment is handled by Google Play; ScoreQuest never sees your card. When you start a purchase, ScoreQuest attaches an identifier it makes for the purpose (a hash of a random number created on your phone) to the purchase, which Google says can help it detect fraud; our server does not receive or store it. The Google Play Billing library that handles purchases inside the app is made by Google and may send Google technical reports about how it is working, for example whether a call succeeded. We do not receive those reports and do not control them.

Android's own backup

If you have backup switched on, Android may copy your learning progress (words learned, answers, practice-test results, your lists, your target score, your settings, your activity record of study time and minutes earned, and the groups you made on the Learning goals screen, which name the apps in them) to a private folder in your Google account, and to a new phone when you move. It does not copy your blocked-app list, your schedules, your wallet, your purchase state or your AI consent. ScoreQuest cannot read or delete that copy; you manage it in Android's backup settings. Android says the copy is protected by your Google Account and is end-to-end encrypted on Android 9 and later when you have a screen lock.

Third parties, children and deleting your data on Android

The Android app contains Google's Play Billing library, Google's Health Connect library and Android's own support libraries. It contains no advertising network, no analytics, no crash reporter and no Firebase SDK. The only other party is OpenAI, when you use the cloud AI, as above.

ScoreQuest is designed for adults studying for an exam. It is not directed to children.

Uninstalling ScoreQuest, or clearing its storage in Android's settings, removes everything stored on your phone. "Reset learning progress" in Settings erases your study history but keeps your word lists, goals, earned minutes, blocked apps, schedules, settings and purchase. A copy made by Android's backup is yours to delete in your phone's backup settings. On our server there is no account to delete, and the records described above are filed under an identifier made from your purchase rather than from your name or email, so we cannot look them up from either; we do not offer a way to request deletion of them.
